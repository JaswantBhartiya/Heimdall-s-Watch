In the Aegis-Wrist project, the INMP441 MEMS microphone serves several critical hardware and software functions centered around real-time safety monitoring, event verification, and system efficiency:

1. Real-Time Acoustic Threat Sensing
Scream and Impact Detection: Continuously samples environmental sound levels to identify sudden acoustic threats, such as human screams, high-decibel spikes, or structural impact sounds.

Acoustic Profiling: Analyzes frequency patterns and sound intensity to help distinguish legitimate distress signals from ordinary background noise, reducing false positives.

2. Audio Snippet Buffering & Forensic Recording
Pre- and Post-Trigger Ring Buffering: Continuously writes audio to a rolling circular memory buffer. When an emergency alert or manual trigger occurs, the system preserves the buffered audio snippet immediately preceding and following the event.

Evidence Preservation: Captures clear audio clips during emergency events to assist in forensic documentation and verification.

3. Direct Digital Interface with ESP32-S3 (I2S)
Direct I2S Streaming: Outputs 24-bit digital pulse-density/PCM audio data over the I2S (Integrated Interchip Sound) bus directly into the ESP32-S3 microcontroller.

Elimination of External ADC: Eliminates the need for an external Analog-to-Digital Converter (ADC), keeping the overall hardware footprint minimal and reducing component count.

4. High Immunity to RF and Electrical Interference
Noise Rejection: As a digital MEMS microphone, the INMP441 converts sound to digital signals right at the sensor capsule, rendering the signal highly immune to electromagnetic interference (EMI) and noise generated by nearby cellular modems, Wi-Fi, or Bluetooth radios on the wristband.

Key Technical Specs Relevant to the Functions
Type: Omnidirectional MEMS digital microphone

Interface: I2S (SCK, WS, SD)

SNR / Sensitivity: High signal-to-noise ratio suitable for far-field audio detection

Power Requirements: Low power consumption suited for wearable battery operation
